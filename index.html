Get comfortable with the format before it counts.

Arctic Shores–style assessments don't have right answers — they watch how you play. These mini-games mirror the mechanics candidates report seeing most often, from memory sequences to probabilistic learning. Play each a few times so the format feels familiar, not the content. Before you play the real thing

  • There's no pass/fail score to chase — these games read behaviour, not "correct" answers, so play at a pace that's genuinely yours rather than a stressed guess-fast pace.
  • Read each task's on-screen instructions fully before starting; the rules often change slightly round to round.
  • Expect distractors. Several games deliberately show information you should ignore — resisting it is part of what's measured.
  • Find somewhere quiet with a stable connection, and try to complete the real assessment in one sitting.
  • Rest before you start. Fatigue shows up in reaction-time and risk-taking data more than people expect.

Sequence

round 1 · best —

Watch the tiles light up, then click them back in the same order. One or two tiles will flash a different colour — those are decoys; ignore them. Sequences get longer as you go.

Direction

round 0/24 · accuracy —

Look only at the middle arrow, dead centre of the grid. Press Q if it points left, P if it points right — the arrows around it point every which way to throw you off, so ignore them. But if every other arrow is swapped for an X, that's a stop signal: don't press anything that round.

Preparation

balloon 0/10

Each pump inflates the balloon and adds to its value. Balloons pop at random — when one pops, its value is lost. Bank at any time to lock in what you've earned so far and move to the next balloon.

Security

round 0/10 · target — · streak 0

The dial is a clock face, numbered 1 to 12. Click "Stop" the instant the needle crosses the highlighted zone at the target number. It doesn't just spin at a constant speed — it can pause and resume, speed up, or reverse direction partway round, often right at one of the numbers, so watch its actual motion rather than expecting a steady rhythm. Miss the zone and the round restarts.

Energy

presses left 30 · generator 1/3

Each press changes the generator's power — sometimes up, sometimes down, following a hidden pattern that resets with each new generator. Learn the rhythm and keep the power from hitting empty.

Team

round 0/12 · you 0 · opponent 0

You and a computer-played team sell the same product and each set a price every round. Both high: you split a large, steady payout. Both low: a small, even one. One high and one low: the low-price team takes everything. Your opponent reacts to how you've been playing.

Ticket

round 0/24 · accuracy —

A shape with a number lands in either the top or the bottom box. In the top box, only the number matters — odd is P, even is Q, ignore the shape. In the bottom box, only the shape matters — rounded is Q, cornered is P, ignore the number. Same keys either way, different rule depending where it lands.

Analysis

round 0/8 · accuracy —

Each round is a small table of cities with an average claim and an average premium. Work out the answer — a calculator and a moment of arithmetic are entirely fair game here.

Weather

round 0/100 · accuracy —

Two symbols flash together. Neither means anything on its own, but certain pairings lean toward day or night more often than not — and that tendency can drift the longer you play, though not for every pairing. Guess Day or Night within 3 seconds; either way, you won't find out which it was until the full 3 seconds are up.
